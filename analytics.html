Of 86,106 official documents read, 79,877 forecasts and commitments reached the public ledger, 43,679 of them clear enough to check, and 497 have a verdict so far.

How to read it: each bar is a step; its length is its count compared with the first step. The line under each number says how much of the step above survives. A long drop between two bars shows where statements about the future stop being checkable.

4. Where is the language most ambiguous?

Most ambiguous subject: Interest rates (average 42 of 100); least: Growth (33).

How to read it: ambiguity counts how many different outcomes would all fit the sentence (0 = exactly one, 100 = very many). Longer bar = more room to claim success afterwards whatever happens.

5. What kind of statements are they?

The most common kind is forecast: 57,840 statements, 72% of the ledger.

How to read it: a forecast says what will happen; a scenario says what would happen if; a commitment says what the speaker will do. Only these kinds are on the public ledger - opinions and calls for action are archived, not scored.

6. How far ahead can forecasters see?

For ECB staff, the average miss on inflation grows from 0.2 points for the current year to 2.0 points 3 years ahead. The further ahead a forecast looks, the less it should be trusted to the decimal.

How to read it: left to right is how many years ahead the forecast looked (0 = the year it was made in); up is the average miss in percentage points. A line that climbs means accuracy fades with distance. The scoreboard has every forecast round behind these averages.

7. What falls due, and when?

7,582 statements on the ledger name a deadline still ahead; the busiest year is 2026 with 2,576.

How to read it: one bar per year in which statements reach their deadline. These are the dates to watch: when a year ends, its statements move to “due” and are checked against official figures.

8. Verdicts so far

Of 497 statements by people checked so far: 182 came true, 81 partly, 234 did not.

How to read it: every verdict is reached automatically, by the published checking rules, against official data; the arithmetic and the evidence link are on the statement's own page.
