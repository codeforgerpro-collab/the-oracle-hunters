Methodology

The framework is Professor Dimitrios Thomakos's: Ars Prognostica — The Origins, Evolution, and Formalization of Prediction (2026) and Intelligence Without Wisdom — Practical Judgment and the Epistemology of AI Risk Claims (2026). The Oracle Hunters applies it; any error in the application is ours.

1. What is collected

Speeches and statements from official, primary sources only: central banks (ECB, Federal Reserve, Bank of England, central bankers via the BIS), governments (GOV.UK, the White House, the Greek Prime Minister's office, the European Commission and the official government sources verified for Proxenos LinkLab) and AI companies' own statements. Each document keeps two UTC timestamps: when it was said or published, and when we captured it. Documents whose publication date cannot be read are marked “first seen”. The first run went back 365 days.

2. Which sentences

From each document we keep up to 6 forward-looking sentences: those that speak about the future (future tense, expectations, projections, horizons, probabilities) or that prescribe action. Procedural sentences (“I will now turn to…”) are excluded.

3. How each statement is classified

Speech act — forecast, scenario, conjecture, strategic announcement, policy prescription, or a combination (six-fold classification).
Claim tier — possibility, mechanism, forecast, policy (four-tier hierarchy: each needs a different kind of evidence).
Axis — empirical (rests on data, figures, projections), abductive (a conjecture or scenario), oracular (asserted on authority, no evidence named).

4. Testability

Four checks: a defined event, a dated horizon, a probability (stated or verbal), a measurable indicator. 4/4 = Testable; 2-3 = Partly testable; 0-1 = Unresolvable as stated. Vague horizons (“over time”, “in the medium term”) do not count.

5. Ambiguity Meter

A practical version of the ambivalence function (entropy over the readings a statement allows): the number of readings grows with hedges (may, could, gradually…) and with each missing element (event, horizon, probability). Ambiguity = log₂(readings), scaled 0-100. It is a proxy, published so that anyone can check it.

7. Resolution and scoring

When a forecast's horizon passes it becomes Due for review. An editor resolves it against official data (came true / partly / did not / unresolvable) and links the evidence. Calibration is the Brier score — the mean of (probability − outcome)², a proper scoring rule, so it rewards honest probabilities rather than confident-sounding ones. A score is shown only after 10 resolved forecasts.

8. U-turns, goalposts, market reaction, AI warnings

Possible U-turns (same speaker, same topic, opposite direction within 180 days) are found automatically but published only after an editor confirms them. Goalpost moves are recorded by an editor. Market reaction shows bond-yield moves around central-bank speeches — context, not causation. AI warnings are put through the six-point public-warrant test.

9. Rules we keep

Primary sources first · the same tests for optimists and pessimists, every party, every country · the original words always next to their classification · labels judge the claim, never the person · a public corrections log and a right of reply · short excerpts with a link to the full source.
