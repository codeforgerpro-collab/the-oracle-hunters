Forecast Scoreboard

The official forecasts of central banks, each kept with the date it was made, next to what actually happened. This is a complete track record: every forecast round since 1999, nothing left out.

How to read this, and why it is here Why: institutions publish numbers with dates, so they can be checked exactly - no interpretation is needed. They are also the benchmark for everyone else: a minister's forecast is only as good as it is better than the central bank's.
How: for each year we take the forecast and subtract the official outcome. “Average miss” is the average size of that gap in percentage points (smaller is better). “Too low / too high” shows whether the misses lean one way (an optimism or pessimism bias). “One year ahead” uses the last forecast made in the year before. Nothing is rounded in the institution's favour and nothing is edited by hand: the numbers come straight from the official databases named under each table.
